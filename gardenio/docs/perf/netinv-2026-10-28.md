# /garden network inventory: baseline, 2026-10-28

P0.4 of the Lands UX performance pass (gd-hsekIVjOWI, plan gd-MEGB3dEaHu). It records every fetch and EventSource of
each /garden surface while idle, while the farm is busy, and across a dropped connection, which is the input to P2.4
(gd-ymB1Pgg0fQ, one EventSource per page). Rerun it with `make garden-netinv OUT=<dir>` (about 40 minutes); the harness
is `gardenio/controller/internal/gardenhub/netinv_browser_test.go` and `testdata/netinv_browser.js`.

**What was measured.** Headless Chromium against cardWorld (the goldens' fixture farm) with the Commons live, on a
real clock, desktop viewport. Each surface was opened the way its golden row opens it, then:

- **idle**: 5 minutes with nothing changing.
- **active**: 5 minutes with a tick every 5 s. Each tick is one gardener tool call and one farm change. Every second
  tick adds a comment on plan A, and every third a #general message. Per minute that is 12 farm patches, 6 bead moves
  and 4 Commons messages, and the World pans every 15 s. Production rates differ, so read the active numbers as cost
  per event, not as a forecast.
- **blip**: every connection cut once, with the server still up. This is a proxy restart or a phone changing
  networks.
- **outage**: every connection cut, then every request answered 502 for 30 s, which is what the edge does while the
  viewer rolls. After that, 60 s to recover.

Bytes are response bodies as the server wrote them, after the page's gzip but before TLS and headers. Load bytes are
mostly the inlined page (about 450 KB; P0.1 owns the load numbers).

## Findings

1. **Every page holds 2 or 3 EventSources.** Every surface keeps `/garden/events` (lists) and `/garden/farm/events`
   (farm). The Commons pane adds `/garden/c/events`, the bead view `/garden/bead/{id}/events`, the cockpit
   `/garden/plan/{id}/events`, the Glass `/garden/glass/{id}/stream` and the Wall `/garden/glass/wall`. Each sends a
   heartbeat every 15 s. Over HTTP/1.1, two tabs with 3 streams each fill Chrome's 6 connections per host, and every
   other fetch then queues. The edge's HTTP version was not checked: it is not reachable from an agent pod.
2. **Idle is within the 5 KB/min budget everywhere**, at 0.9 to 1.4 KB/min. But two polls run on top of the streams:
   - `GET /garden/c/unread` every 60 s on every surface without the Commons pane open. That is about 0.7 KB/min, and
     the farm stream could carry it instead.
   - `GET /garden/c/{land}/{channel}/typing` every 7 s for as long as a typing line shows: 43 requests in 5 minutes.
     The fixture keeps flo typing forever, so this is the worst case, but production polls the same way while anyone
     types.
3. **Active cost comes from a refetch on every event, not from the streams.**
   - The farm stream itself is about 5.3 KB/min at 12 patches a minute: a patch is about 450 B, plus a `busy` event
     per tick.
   - The bead view and cockpit refetch their whole document (about 3.4 KB) on every `moved` event. That is 20 KB/min
     of their 26.6 KB/min.
   - The project panel refetches its whole project (3.3 KB) every 20 s while the farm moves.
   - The Commons pane makes 5 requests for each message: the whole channel list `GET /garden/c` (4.4 KB), the message
     fragment, the unread count twice (once after the RECOUNT_MS debounce and once after its own read mark), and the
     read mark. That is 32.4 KB/min at 4 messages a minute, and the list refetch is 45% of it.
4. **A blip is handled well, apart from its timing.** Every stream reconnected after exactly 2.0 s (the server's
   `retry: 2000`). `/garden/c/events` took 3.0 s: it sends no `retry:` line, so Chromium's default applies. There is no
   backoff and no jitter, so every viewer reconnects in the same 2-second window after a restart. Resume worked: there
   were 0 duplicate events and no `reset` on any stream, because Last-Event-ID or `?since=` covers the gap. The
   re-snapshot was small:
   - the bead view and cockpit fetch their document twice;
   - the project panel fetches once;
   - the Commons fetches its list, two messages, the unread count twice, the typing line twice, and a read mark;
   - the World, queues, Glass and Wall send only their viewcut POST.
5. **An outage (a 502 on reconnect) is the real gap.** Chromium's EventSource treats a non-200 as fatal: it closes and
   never retries.
   - `/garden/events` and `/garden/farm/events` came back only through the page's 45-second watchdogs (garden.js,
     plans.js). That was 3 to 17 s after the server returned, and up to about 47 s after the cut.
   - **The Commons, bead-view, cockpit, Glass and Wall streams never came back.**
   - The bead view and cockpit fall back to a 60 s poll (workbench.js `FLOOR_MS`).
   - The Glass and Wall say they are unavailable and keep the last screen.
   - The Commons pane goes dark without saying so: its closed `es` stays non-null, so `connect()` does nothing (same
     spot), and the 60 s unread poll, which runs only `if (!es)`, stops too. Until the viewer changes channel or hides
     and shows the tab, nothing new arrives. Filed as gd-bo9Yp4o82X.
   - None of this storms: each stream made only 1 or 2 attempts.

## What this means for P2.4

Done in P2.4: [`one-eventsource-2026-10-29.md`](one-eventsource-2026-10-29.md) has the change and the rerun.

- **One shared stream per page.** Fold the panel streams (bead, plan, Commons, Glass, Wall) and the two base streams
  into a single connection that carries named channels. A panel then subscribes and unsubscribes on that connection
  (as viewcut already does for the farm) instead of opening its own.
- **Reconnect in the page's own code, not with native retry.** Use exponential backoff with full jitter (for example
  1 s up to 30 s) on both error paths: a dropped stream, and a refused one (5xx, 401). Resume from the last event id,
  which the server already honours on every stream.
- **Retire the polls the stream can cover:**
  - unread counts, as a stream event;
  - the typing line, which the `typing` event could carry as text;
  - the project panel's 20 s refetch, as a project patch;
  - the bead view's floor poll, once reconnect is reliable.
- **Patch rather than refetch on `moved` and `message`:**
  - a Commons message should not refetch the whole channel list;
  - a bead move could carry the changed sections.
  This is where the active bytes go.
